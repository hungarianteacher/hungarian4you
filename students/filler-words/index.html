Töltelékszavak

A magyar beszélt nyelv apró, jelentés nélküli — mégis nélkülözhetetlen — szavai.
The small words that don't add dictionary content, but carry hesitation, emphasis, hedging and structure in natural Hungarian speech.

At C1 level, grammar and vocabulary are rarely the bottleneck — naturalness is. One of the fastest ways to close that last gap is mastering töltelékszavak ("filling words"): the small words and phrases that don't add dictionary content but carry hesitation, emphasis, hedging, politeness, and structure. Native Hungarian speech — and a surprising amount of semi-formal writing — is threaded through with them. This guide collects 47 of them, grouped by what each word actually does in a sentence, with pronunciation, register, and real example sentences, so you can reach for the right one instead of defaulting to hát and szóval every time.

A note on register before you dive in: each word below is tagged casual (friends, family, informal chat), neutral (works almost everywhere, spoken and written), or formal (lectures, essays, interviews, professional speech). At C1, choosing the right register is often more important than choosing the right word.

01 Tiszta hezitációs hangok — Pure hesitation sounds

These carry no meaning at all — they simply hold the floor while the speaker thinks. Every language has them; Hungarian's just sound different from English's "um" and "uh."

ő…≈ uh…
pronounced roughly “a drawn-out "ur" sound”

The most neutral hesitation sound, roughly Hungarian's version of English "uh." Purely acoustic — no grammatical role at all. casual

Ez… ő… nem is olyan egyszerű.This… uh… isn't so simple.
öö≈ um…
pronounced roughly “elongated "ur", often nasal”

Slightly longer and more drawn out than ő, used when the pause is longer — searching harder for a word or an idea. casual

Öö, hogy is kezdjem…Um, how should I start this…
hm / hmm≈ hmm
pronounced roughly “a closed-mouth hum”

A thinking sound, often before a considered answer or a mild objection — the same function it has in English. neutral

Hm, ezen még gondolkodnom kell.Hmm, I need to think about that.
khm≈ ahem
pronounced roughly “a small throat-clear”

A stalling cough-like sound, sometimes used self-consciously before saying something awkward or before correcting oneself. casual

Khm, szóval erről lenne szó.Ahem, so, this is what it's about.
izé≈ thingy / whatchamacallit / um
pronounced roughly “EE-zeh”

Hungarian's most famous filler — a genuine placeholder that can stand in for almost any noun or verb the speaker can't retrieve, as well as a pure hesitation sound. Ubiquitous in speech, avoided in writing. casual

Add ide azt az izét! Nem, most nem izélek, dolgozom.Hand me that thingy! No, I'm not doing... that thing right now, I'm working.

02 Beszédindítók — Conversation openers

These sit at the very front of a sentence and signal that a response, a story, or a new thought is coming.

hát≈ well… / so…
pronounced roughly “haht”

The single most frequent Hungarian filler. Opens almost any kind of sentence, hedging, softening, or simply buying time before the real content arrives. neutral

Hát, nem is tudom, mit mondjak.Well, I don't really know what to say.
nos≈ well, then…
pronounced roughly “nosh”

A more formal, almost literary cousin of hát. Common in interviews, presentations, and written dialogue rather than everyday chat. formal

Nos, kezdjük az elején.Well then, let's start from the beginning.
na≈ well / alright / c'mon
pronounced roughly “nah”

A short, punchy attention-getter or reaction word, often carrying mild impatience, resignation, or encouragement depending on tone. casual

Na, akkor induljunk.Alright then, let's get going.
namost / na most≈ now then / so now
pronounced roughly “nah-MOSHT”

Combines na and most ("now") to mark a transition — often used when moving from small talk to the actual point. neutral

Namost, térjünk a tárgyra.Now then, let's get to the point.
hát akkor≈ so then / well then
pronounced roughly “haht AH-kor”

A combination that signals a conclusion or a decision is coming after some back-and-forth. neutral

Hát akkor, holnap találkozunk.So then, we'll meet tomorrow.

03 Logikai kötőszavak, töltelékként — Logical connectors used as filler

These are "real" words with genuine logical meaning, but in fast speech they're often bleached into near-empty connectors — very close to how English overuses "basically" or "essentially." C1 learners should know both the literal and the filler use.

szóval≈ so / so basically
pronounced roughly “SOH-vahl”

Literally "with word(s)." The default way to summarize, restart a thought, or move a story forward — Hungarian's all-purpose "so." neutral

Szóval, ahogy mondtam, ma nem érek rá.So, like I said, I'm not free today.
tehát≈ so / therefore
pronounced roughly “TEH-haht”

A logical connector that sounds more organized than szóval — the "therefore" of casual "so." Common in lectures, arguments, and written prose. formal

A bolt zárva van, tehát holnap kell visszamennünk.The shop is closed, so we'll have to go back tomorrow.
eszerint≈ according to this / so it seems
pronounced roughly “ES-eh-rint”

Literally "according to this." Used to draw a conclusion from what was just said — slightly more formal and precise than szóval. formal

Eszerint mégsem jön el.So it seems he's not coming after all.
tulajdonképpen≈ actually / strictly speaking
pronounced roughly “TOO-lai-don-kayp-pen”

Marks a correction, clarification, or a truth that's slightly different from what was implied — very close to English "actually" as a discourse marker. neutral

Tulajdonképpen igazad van.Actually, you're right.
voltaképpen≈ in fact / essentially
pronounced roughly “VOAL-tah-kayp-pen”

A more literary sibling of tulajdonképpen, common in essays, journalism, and careful spoken argument. formal

Voltaképpen ugyanarról van szó.In fact, it's the same thing.
lényegében≈ essentially / basically
pronounced roughly “LAY-nyeg-eh-ben”

From lényeg ("essence"). Signals that what follows is a simplified, core version of a more complex reality. neutral

Lényegében egyetértünk.We essentially agree.
gyakorlatilag≈ basically / practically
pronounced roughly “DYAH-kor-lah-tee-log”

Literally "practically speaking." Has drifted into filler territory among younger and educated speakers, much like English "basically" is overused today. neutral

Gyakorlatilag kész vagyok, csak indulnom kell.I'm basically ready, I just need to head out.

04 Óvatossági szavak, finomítók — Hedges and softeners

These soften a claim, signal uncertainty, or leave room for being wrong — an important register skill at C1, where sounding too blunt or too certain can feel unnatural in Hungarian conversation.

mondjuk≈ well, actually / let's say
pronounced roughly “MOAN-dyook”

Literally "let's say," from mondani (to say). As a genuine phrase it introduces an example; as a filler it softly introduces a counterpoint or afterthought. neutral

Mondjuk, igazad is lehet.Well, actually, you might be right.
gondolom≈ I guess / I suppose
pronounced roughly “GON-doh-lom”

Literally "I think it." Softens a statement into a guess, often used when the speaker is fairly confident but wants to leave room for correction. neutral

Gondolom, ezt te is tudtad.I guess you knew this too.
valahogy≈ somehow / kind of
pronounced roughly “VAH-lah-hoy”

Literally "somehow." Used to hedge a feeling or judgment that's hard to pin down precisely. neutral

Ez valahogy furcsának tűnik.This somehow feels strange.
bizonyos értelemben≈ in a sense
pronounced roughly “BEE-zoh-nyosh EHR-teh-lem-ben”

A more academic hedge, used to qualify a claim as true only from a certain angle — common in essays, debates, and careful spoken argument. formal

Bizonyos értelemben mindketten igazat mondanak.In a sense, they're both telling the truth.
amolyan≈ a sort of / a kind of
pronounced roughly “AH-moh-lyahn”

Hedges a description that isn't quite exact — similar to "a sort of" before a noun in English. neutral

Ez amolyan félig kész terv.It's a sort of half-finished plan.
egyfajta≈ a kind of / a type of
pronounced roughly “EDJ-fai-tah”

Very close to amolyan, literally "a kind of." Slightly more common in writing and careful speech. neutral

Egyfajta megkönnyebbülést éreztem.I felt a kind of relief.

05 Megerősítést kereső szavak — Tag words and confirmation-seekers

These invite the listener into the conversation, checking agreement or shared knowledge — a very Hungarian conversational habit that keeps dialogue feeling collaborative.

ugye≈ right? / isn't it / you know
pronounced roughly “OO-dyeh”

A tag word seeking agreement or confirmation, like English "right?" — but also dropped mid-sentence just to keep the listener engaged, with no direct English equivalent in that position. neutral

Te is jössz a buliba, ugye?You're coming to the party too, right?
tudod≈ you know
pronounced roughly “TOO-dohd”

Literally "you know" (from tudni, to know). Used exactly like the English filler — checking the listener is following, or softening a personal remark. casual

Tudod, nem is olyan egyszerű ez az egész.You know, this whole thing isn't so simple.
na ugye≈ see, right? / told you
pronounced roughly “nah OO-dyeh”

Combines na and ugye into a satisfied "I told you so" — used when something confirms what the speaker predicted. casual

Na ugye, megmondtam, hogy esni fog!See, right? I told you it would rain!

06 Elnagyoló, listát lezáró kifejezések — Approximators and trailing expressions

These trail off a list or a description rather than finishing it precisely — Hungarian's answer to English "and stuff," "or whatever," and "etc."

meg minden≈ and everything / and all that
pronounced roughly “meg MIN-den”

Tacked onto the end of a list to imply there's more, unspecified, similar content. casual

Vittünk sátrat, hálózsákot, meg minden.We brought a tent, a sleeping bag, and everything.
meg ilyenek≈ and such things / and stuff like that
pronounced roughly “meg EE-lyen-ek”

Very close to meg minden, literally "and such ones." casual

Szeretek főzni, sütni, meg ilyenek.I like cooking, baking, and stuff like that.
vagy ilyesmi≈ or something like that
pronounced roughly “vaj EE-lyesh-mee”

Softens a guess or an approximate description, similar to English "or something." casual

Azt hiszem, orvos vagy ilyesmi.I think he's a doctor or something like that.
vagy mi≈ or whatever
pronounced roughly “vaj mee”

A blunter, more dismissive version of vagy ilyesmi — literally "or what." casual

Menjünk moziba, vagy mi.Let's go to the movies, or whatever.
ilyesmi≈ something like this/that
pronounced roughly “EE-lyesh-mee”

A standalone approximator, often closing a description that the speaker doesn't want to spell out fully. neutral

Egy kis félreértés történt, ilyesmi.There was a small misunderstanding, something like that.
satöbbi (stb.)≈ etcetera
pronounced roughly “SHAH-teub-bee”

The written-friendly "etc." — less a spoken filler and more a list-closer used in both speech and writing, unlike the more casual options above. formal

Kell hozzá liszt, cukor, tojás, satöbbi.You need flour, sugar, eggs, etcetera.

07 Újrafogalmazó kifejezések — Reformulation markers

These buy time while the speaker searches for a better way to say something, or flag that a phrase is deliberately loose. Several of them — úgymond, mondhatni — are common in more formal or written C1-level Hungarian.

hogy is mondjam≈ how should I put it
pronounced roughly “hoy ish MOAN-dyahm”

A classic stalling phrase used while searching for the right word, especially before saying something delicate. Pairs naturally with hát or izé. neutral

Ő, hogy is mondjam, kicsit különc.He is, how should I put it, a bit eccentric.
hogy úgy mondjam≈ so to speak
pronounced roughly “hoy oog MOAN-dyahm”

Literally "so that I put it that way." Marks a phrase as figurative or slightly unusual, much like English "so to speak." neutral

A projekt, hogy úgy mondjam, hajszálon múlt.The project, so to speak, hung by a thread.
úgymond≈ so to speak / as it were
pronounced roughly “OOG-moand”

A single-word, more literary equivalent of hogy úgy mondjam — very common in essays, journalism, and formal speech. formal

A cég, úgymond, új korszakba lépett.The company has, so to speak, entered a new era.
mondhatni≈ one might say / you could say
pronounced roughly “MOND-haht-nee”

Literally "one can say." Marks a slightly bold or figurative claim as reasonable, often in written or semi-formal Hungarian. formal

A film, mondhatni, korszakalkotó volt.The film was, one might say, groundbreaking.
jobban mondva≈ or rather / to put it better
pronounced roughly “YOB-bahn MOND-vah”

Used to correct or sharpen a previous statement mid-sentence — "or rather" in English. formal

Sietnünk kell, jobban mondva, most azonnal indulnunk kell.We need to hurry — or rather, we need to leave right now.

08 Témaváltó kifejezések — Topic shifters

These signal a turn in the conversation — a new topic, an aside, or a return to the main thread.

amúgy≈ by the way / anyway
pronounced roughly “AH-mooj”

Used to change topics, add an aside, or steer a conversation back on track — the Hungarian cousin of "anyway" or "by the way." neutral

Amúgy, láttad már az új filmet?By the way, have you seen the new film yet?
egyébként≈ by the way / incidentally
pronounced roughly “EH-djeb-kent”

A slightly more formal alternative to amúgy, common in both speech and writing. formal

Egyébként köszönöm a segítséget.By the way, thank you for the help.
apropó≈ speaking of which
pronounced roughly “AH-proh-poh”

Borrowed from French à propos. Introduces a topic triggered by something just mentioned. neutral

Apropó utazás, foglaltál már jegyet?Speaking of the trip, have you booked a ticket yet?

09 Lezáró, belenyugvó kifejezések — Closing and resignation markers

These wrap up a topic, concede a point, or signal that the speaker is moving on, sometimes with a hint of reluctance.

na jó≈ well, okay / fine then
pronounced roughly “nah yoh”

A soft concession — closing off a topic or grudgingly agreeing, similar to sighing "well, okay" or "fine" in English. casual

Na jó, legyen a te igazad.Alright, fine, have it your way.
na mindegy≈ anyway, never mind
pronounced roughly “nah MIN-degy”

Signals the speaker is dropping a point rather than pursuing it further. casual

Na mindegy, majd holnap megbeszéljük.Anyway, never mind, we'll talk about it tomorrow.
hát azért≈ well, because… / still
pronounced roughly “haht AH-zairt”

Introduces a mild but unresolved objection — the speaker concedes a point while still holding onto a reservation. neutral

Hát azért ez elég furcsa döntés volt.Well, still, that was a pretty strange decision.

10 Reakciószavak, indulatszók — Reaction words and interjections

Short, reflexive words that react to what was just said — agreement, mild surprise, or pushback.

persze≈ of course / sure
pronounced roughly “PEHR-seh”

A genuine word ("of course") that also works as a quick filler of agreement, often used to fill a beat before continuing. neutral

Persze, hát ez világos.Of course, well, that's obvious.
jé≈ oh! / huh, look at that
pronounced roughly “yay”

A short interjection of mild surprise, often at something small and unexpected. casual

Jé, de rég láttalak!Oh! It's been so long since I've seen you!
ja / hát ja≈ oh, yeah / right, yeah
pronounced roughly “yah / haht yah”

A casual acknowledgment, often marking that the speaker just remembered or realized something. casual

Ja, tényleg, elfelejtettem szólni.Oh, right, I forgot to mention it.
na ne már≈ come on now / you're kidding
pronounced roughly “nah neh mahr”

An informal reaction of mild disbelief or protest — closer to English "come on" than to a true filler, but extremely common in spoken Hungarian. casual

Na ne már, ezt most komolyan mondod?Come on now, are you serious?

● Minta-párbeszéd — Sample dialogue

A short, casual exchange showing many of these words layered together the way they actually occur in speech — natives rarely use just one at a time.

Kata: Hát, nem is tudom, izé, elmenjünk-e ma este.

Well, I don't know, um, if we should go out tonight.

Peti: Szóval te nem akarsz jönni, ugye?

So you don't want to come, right?

Kata: Mondjuk, fáradt vagyok, de na jó, menjünk.

Well actually, I'm tired, but fine, let's go.

Peti: Na, ez az! Amúgy, tudod, ismered az új helyet a sarkon?

Alright, that's the spirit! By the way, you know, do you know the new place on the corner?

Kata: Hogy is mondjam… gyakorlatilag minden nap arra járok, meg minden.

How should I put it… I basically walk by there every day, and everything.

Peti: Na ne már, ezt eddig miért nem mondtad?!

Come on now, why didn't you say so before?!

● Regiszter és stílus — Register and style at C1

Spoken vs. written Hungarian

Most of the words above belong to spoken Hungarian and should be avoided in formal writing (essays, official letters, reports). A handful — úgymond, mondhatni, tulajdonképpen, voltaképpen, lényegében, bizonyos értelemben — cross over comfortably into semi-formal and even academic writing, which makes them especially valuable at C1: they let you hedge and reformulate in an essay the way a native writer would, rather than leaning on English-influenced phrasing.

Stacking fillers

Notice in the dialogue above that speakers rarely use a single filler at a time — hát, izé or hogy is mondjam… gyakorlatilag pile up naturally. As a learner, resist the urge to stack more than two; native-like stacking comes with exposure, and overdoing it early can sound like stalling rather than natural hesitation.

Regional and generational variation

Some fillers skew younger or more urban (gyakorlatilag, amolyan as a filler), while others are heard across all generations (hát, szóval, na). Formal markers like voltaképpen and úgymond are more common among educated, Budapest-influenced speech and in the media than in casual small-town conversation — worth bearing in mind if you're modeling your Hungarian on a specific speaker or region.

● Tanácsok a természetes használathoz — Tips for sounding natural

  • Build outward from the essentials. If you only add three, make them hát, szóval, and ugye — they're neutral, extremely frequent, and hard to overuse.
  • Match register to context. Save izé, na, na jó, and na ne már for friends and casual settings; reach for tehát, voltaképpen, and úgymond in presentations, interviews, or essays.
  • Use hedges to sound more Hungarian, not less confident. Words like mondjuk, gondolom, and bizonyos értelemben are not signs of weak language skills — native speakers use them constantly to sound thoughtful rather than blunt.
  • Don't overload one sentence. Native speakers space fillers out; stacking three or four in a row can sound like you're stalling rather than speaking naturally.
  • Read and listen actively. Interviews, podcasts, and talk shows are full of the formal-register markers (tulajdonképpen, lényegében, mondhatni); everyday conversation and vlogs are better for the casual set (izé, na, meg minden).
  • Use ugye to sound engaged. Dropping it into a sentence signals you expect the listener to agree or already know what you mean — a very Hungarian conversational habit that has no single English equivalent.
  • In writing, prune ruthlessly. Even the formal-register words should be used sparingly in essays — one or two per page is natural; more starts to read as padding, exactly as overusing "basically" or "essentially" would in English academic writing.
